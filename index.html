Acknowledgement of Country

Sovereignty was never ceded. No treaty was ever signed. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples are the Traditional Custodians of the lands and waters on which this work is made, and that does not become less true with time, legislation or silence.

FREE 2B ME acknowledges Elders past, present and emerging. This body of work exists because systems cause harm when they ask people to become easier to accommodate. No community in this country has been asked to do that more often, or for longer, than First Nations peoples.

We hold that as a matter of practice, not sentiment.

Maybe you know this feeling

You got through it.

But what did “fine” cost?

What people saw

  • Made it to work.
  • Joined the meeting.
  • Answered the messages.
  • Went to dinner.
  • Got through the day.

What they didn’t see

  • Preparation.
  • Masking.
  • Pain.
  • Sensory load.
  • Rehearsing.
  • Support.
  • Cancelled plans.
  • Recovery afterwards.

Functioning tells us something happened.
It doesn’t tell us what it cost.

Being able to endure something is not proof that it works for you.

A question to take with you

Does this leave room for me to be me?

  1. In support

    Can I say no, disagree, communicate differently and still belong?

  2. At work

    Can I contribute without performing someone else’s version of professional?

  3. In relationships

    Can I be known without becoming manageable?

  4. In healthcare

    Can I need support without losing authority?

  5. In community

    Can I participate differently and still count as participating?

  6. In my own life

    Am I building something I can actually inhabit?

FREE 2B ME isn’t one answer. Sometimes it starts with a better question.

Why I built this

Tanya Hicks

Human Systems Architect.

I got tired of belonging being something people had to earn by disappearing.

As a late-identified AuDHD woman living with disability, chronic illness and trauma, and as a single mother of a transgender daughter, I know what it costs to spend years trying to become easier for the world to hold.

I stopped trying to fix myself into acceptability and began building what I could not find: places, practices and pathways where people could belong without becoming less of themselves.

My story is about turning lived experience into infrastructure so our daughters inherit more than encouragement. They inherit different possibilities.

I want them to inherit more room.

  • Room to know themselves.
  • Room to need people.
  • Room to communicate differently.
  • Room to change their minds.
  • Room to love things intensely.
  • Room to participate differently.
  • Room to build lives we could not yet imagine for them.

Not permission to be themselves.

A world in which they don’t need permission.
