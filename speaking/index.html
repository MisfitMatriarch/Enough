Acknowledgement of Country

Sovereignty was never ceded. No treaty was ever signed. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples are the Traditional Custodians of the lands and waters on which this work is made, and that does not become less true with time, legislation or silence.

FREE 2B ME acknowledges Elders past, present and emerging. This body of work exists because systems cause harm when they ask people to become easier to accommodate. No community in this country has been asked to do that more often, or for longer, than First Nations peoples.

We hold that as a matter of practice, not sentiment.

by Tanya Hicks

Signature keynote

FREE 2B ME: Less Fixing. More Living.

You don’t have to become less of yourself to have more of a life. This talk looks at the energy people spend judging, hiding or squashing parts of themselves, why so many settings ask them to, and what becomes possible when all of you is welcome in the room.

Who it suits

  • Conferences and leadership events
  • Workplaces and teams
  • Disability, health and community services
  • Women’s events and community gatherings
  • Professional forums

The central idea

People are often asked to fix themselves into acceptability before they are allowed more of a life. This talk makes room for the whole person: creativity, ambition, sensitivity, contradictions, difficulties, disability, support and joy. That holds for the people being supported and the people doing the supporting.

Format and access

Delivered as a keynote or conference session, shaped around your audience and purpose. Duration and format are agreed with you during planning.

Speaking, listening, writing, watching or taking a break all count as participation. Raise your audience’s access needs when you enquire so they can be built into the plan.

What participants take away

  1. Recognising themselves in what they hear.
  2. Questioning what they keep squashing or hiding.
  3. Naming something they want to make room for.

What becomes possible when people don’t have to become less of themselves to belong?

That question runs through my work.

  • Disability.
  • Leadership.
  • Support.
  • Work.
  • Professional practice.
  • Parenting.
  • Community.
  • Systems.

And the ordinary places where people are asked, often quietly, to spend enormous amounts of themselves being acceptable enough to participate.

FREE 2B ME is about making more room for the person.

The talks

Talks, by the question they leave behind.

  1. What did “fine” cost?

    Visible functioning can hide preparation, masking, pain, support, recovery and effort nobody counted.

    What changes when we stop treating “they managed” as the whole account?

  2. What does our practice require the person to become?

    A question for professionals, services and organisations about the hidden entry requirements inside apparently good support.

    • Calmer?
    • More verbal?
    • More independent?
    • More compliant?
    • Easier to understand?
    • Less inconvenient?

    And what happens when those expectations start being mistaken for progress?

  3. Can I say no and still belong?

    Choice means more when disagreement does not quietly threaten support, relationship, employment, participation or belonging.

    A talk about authorship, consent, power and what it actually means to leave room for another person.

  4. Who is carrying the cost of this working?

    A service can look efficient because somebody else is absorbing the strain. A workplace can look productive because employees are recovering privately. A family can appear to be coping because one person never stops compensating.

    Functioning tells us something happened. It does not tell us what it cost.

  5. What happens when belonging stops requiring performance?

    What would workplaces, schools, services and communities look like if people could contribute without disappearing inside the effort?

    • Not identical participation.
    • Not identical communication.
    • Not identical capacity.

    Belonging with room for difference.

  6. Does this leave room for me to be me?

    A deceptively simple question for support, relationships, workplaces, healthcare, community and life.

    Not: does everybody get everything they want?

    But: can people remain recognisably themselves while being supported, challenged, treated, employed, included and loved?

The point is not to leave agreeing with me. The point is to leave noticing differently.

A good question should keep working after the speaker has left the room.

That is what I am interested in.

  • A sentence in a case note.
  • A person who always seems fine.
  • A policy everyone calls inclusive.
  • A support plan nobody has questioned for years.
  • The employee who delivers brilliantly and disappears afterwards.
  • The person described as “resistant”.
  • The child everyone is helping.
  • The meeting where the person being discussed has somehow disappeared from the account.

For conference organisers

You are not booking a topic list. You are bringing an idea into the room.

Together, we can shape the emphasis around:

  • belonging and identity
  • hidden effort and capacity
  • disability and neurodivergence
  • support without takeover
  • leadership and organisational conditions
  • professional practice and accountability
  • workplaces where contribution and difference can coexist
  • lived experience shaping how things are built

The strongest talks usually begin with the question you want people still discussing afterwards.
