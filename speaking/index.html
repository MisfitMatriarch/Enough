Signature keynote

FREE 2B ME: Less Fixing. More Living.

You don’t have to become less of yourself to have more of a life.

This talk looks at the energy people spend judging, hiding or squashing parts of themselves. It makes room for the whole person: creativity, ambition, sensitivity, difficulties, support and joy.

What participants take away

  1. Notice where they spend energy hiding or pushing through a challenge.
  2. Identify one change that could make the challenge easier to accommodate.
  3. Name a strength or source of joy they want to give more room to.
Audience, format & access

Who it suits

Conferences, leadership events, workplaces, professional forums, disability, health and community services, women’s events and community gatherings.

Shape it for your audience

A keynote or conference session, with duration and format agreed during planning. Speaking, listening, writing, watching or taking a break all count as participation. Share your audience’s access needs so they can be built into the plan.

Also in development · New keynote concept

Life Deals the Cards.
Learn to Play Yours Right.

Ask about this concept ↗

You don’t always get to choose the cards life deals you. This concept draws on therapy, neuroscience-informed education, yoga, mindfulness and lived experience to explore what a winning hand can look like for you.

  • Understand your hand

    Recognise a strength you can use and a challenge you can accommodate.

  • Know what to keep

    Notice when a strategy still serves you, when to try another and when to walk away.

  • Back yourself

    Define winning on your terms and choose a next step, including the support that could make it possible.

Tailored conversations

Questions we can explore.

Start with the question you want people still discussing afterwards. Open a question to explore its focus.

A good question should keep working after the speaker has left the room.

  1. What did “fine” cost?

    Visible functioning can hide preparation, masking, pain, support, recovery and effort nobody counted.

    What changes when we stop treating “they managed” as the whole account?

  2. What does our practice require the person to become?

    A question for professionals, services and organisations about the hidden entry requirements inside apparently good support.

    • Calmer?
    • More verbal?
    • More independent?
    • More compliant?
    • Easier to understand?
    • Less inconvenient?

    And what happens when those expectations start being mistaken for progress?

  3. Can I say no and still belong?

    Choice means more when disagreement does not quietly threaten support, relationship, employment, participation or belonging.

    A talk about authorship, consent, power and what it actually means to leave room for another person.

  4. Who is carrying the cost of this working?

    A service can look efficient because somebody else is absorbing the strain. A workplace can look productive because employees are recovering privately. A family can appear to be coping because one person never stops compensating.

    Functioning tells us something happened. It does not tell us what it cost.

  5. What happens when belonging stops requiring performance?

    What would workplaces, schools, services and communities look like if people could contribute without disappearing inside the effort?

    • Not identical participation.
    • Not identical communication.
    • Not identical capacity.

    Belonging with room for difference.

  6. Does this leave room for me to be me?

    A deceptively simple question for support, relationships, workplaces, healthcare, community and life.

    Not: does everybody get everything they want?

    But: can people remain recognisably themselves while being supported, challenged, treated, employed, included and loved?
