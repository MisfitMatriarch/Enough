Practitioner Pathway

Knowing the right words is not enough.

A person can complete neuroaffirming training, use every correct term, and still practise badly. Language is the easiest part to acquire and the least reliable evidence that anything has changed.

The Practitioner Pathway exists because learning something is not the same as being able to do it well.

The pathway

Five stages, in order

Each stage does something the one before it cannot. Nobody skips ahead by knowing the material.

Foundation Learning

The shared ground: communication, relationships, sensory and environmental access, evidence and assessment, neurodivergence, mental health, trauma and safeguarding, medication and AOD literacy, multidisciplinary practice, scope and professional authority.

Supervised Applied Practice

Using the Practice in real work, with supervision, where the situation is not tidy and the answer is not in the notes.

Observed Practice & Feedback

Someone watches the actual practice and says what they saw. Self-report is not evidence.

Capability Evidence

Demonstrated reasoning against real situations, rather than attendance, completion or a certificate.

Authorised Practice

Scope that reflects what has actually been demonstrated, and what has not.

What makes it different

Learning, application, feedback, reasoning and scope

Most training stops at the first stage and issues a certificate. The Pathway treats that as the beginning.

Practitioners also examine their own assumptions privately. That reflection is theirs: private work is never surveillance, and it is never stored as evidence about them.

Completion records learning. It does not establish competence, readiness to work with a particular person, or professional authority. Those decisions use other evidence.

Where to go from here

Read the Practice first if you have not. The Pathway is how the Practice becomes accountable, so the Practice is the thing it is accountable to.

Professional Learning covers workshops, supervision and team development for people not entering the full Pathway.
