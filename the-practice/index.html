The FREE 2B ME Practice

Understand before you intervene.

FREE 2B ME is a neuroaffirming practice for working out what is happening, what matters to the person, where useful change belongs and who has the responsibility and authority to act.

It begins with the person’s own knowledge of their life. It can involve support, skills, communication, relationships, health, professional intervention, environment, organisations and systems.

The person is not automatically the problem. The environment is not automatically the answer.

Less fixing. More living.

The question

Where does the useful work actually belong?

The question is not simply “How do we change this person?” It is: what needs to change, develop, be supported, be understood or be redesigned — and who has the responsibility and authority to act?

A person may want to build a skill. A relationship may need to change. Communication may need to become more accessible. Health may need assessment or treatment. Practical support may be needed. A practitioner may need a different intervention. An environment or organisation may be creating the barrier.

Often more than one is true.

Capability

Capability without conformity

Less fixing does not mean no learning, no challenge, no development and no intervention.

People may want to build communication, planning, self-advocacy, decision-making, practical capability, relationship skills, ways of navigating distress and change, support-seeking, confidence or other personally valuable skills.

The boundary is not whether the person changes. The boundary is why.

A skill matters when it creates useful options in the person’s life — not merely when it makes them appear more typical, compliant, calm, independent or convenient.

The practice process

Notice → Separate → Locate → Make Accessible → Choose Direction → Support & Implement → Review → Recalibrate

1. Notice

What is actually happening?

2. Separate

Separate observation from interpretation, the person from the behaviour, the goal from the assumed method, and the support need from the service label.

3. Locate

Where does the useful work actually sit?

4. Make Accessible

What needs to become possible before more is asked of the person?

5. Choose Direction

What would genuinely help?

6. Support & Implement

Do the agreed work, within scope.

7. Review

What changed? What did the person experience? Did it help? What did it cost?

8. Recalibrate

Keep it, change it, reduce it, increase it, stop it or try another direction.

This is not a programme people progress through. It is a reasoning process used to keep practice individual, accessible, purposeful and accountable.

Locating the work

Where the work can sit

The work may sit in the person’s skills or capability; communication; health and body considerations; relationships; direct support; professional practice; the environment; the organisation; the wider system; or several places together.

Responsibility should follow authority. Understanding a problem does not make the person responsible for solving every part of it.

Access

Access before intervention

Before interpreting difficulty as unwillingness, incapacity, resistance or behaviour requiring change, ask whether the person can meaningfully access what is being asked — communication, information, relationship, sensory and physical access, timing, support and modality.

Access is not the absence of challenge. Once access is present, meaningful learning, treatment, skill development and challenge may still be appropriate where they serve the person’s goals.

Conditions

Six conditions we pay attention to

Access · Safety · Choice · Voice · Understanding · Partnership

They are areas practitioners consider because they can materially affect what becomes possible. They can be looked at across personal, interactional and structural levels.

They are one object of practice, not the whole Practice.

Authority

Where sovereignty still matters

Sovereignty is the principle that support should increase rather than displace a person’s influence over their own life.

It does not mean doing everything independently. It does not mean refusing professional knowledge or support. It means that support, expertise and systems should not quietly become the author of the person’s life.

The opposite of survival isn’t independence. It’s sovereignty.

Body and access

Nervous System Nourishment

Nervous System Nourishment is one body-and-access framework within FREE 2B ME — not the explanation for the whole Practice. It can explore sensory, movement, rest, communication, relational, environmental and practical factors that may affect access and wellbeing.

It must not become a claim that all behaviour or distress is explained by one nervous-system state, and it does not make calm the goal.

In practice

Good support does not begin by deciding what is wrong with someone

It begins with a fuller account. What does the person know about their own life? What matters to them? What is happening? What skills and support are already present? What needs to become more accessible? What might need to develop? What belongs to a relationship, professional, organisation or system? What evidence would tell us whether the support actually helped?

Begin with the person. Add what is useful. Put responsibility where it belongs. Review what happens.

Less fixing. More living.
