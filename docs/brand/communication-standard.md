# FREE 2B ME communication standard

Governing doctrine. Applies to books, websites, courses, tools, practitioner learning,
posters, social media, presentations, forms, diagrams and video.

> People should not have to work hard to understand work that is supposed to make life
> more workable.

- We use familiar language first.
- We make one point at a time.
- We explain specialist language when it matters.
- We do not confuse complexity with intelligence.
- We do not remove complexity where complexity is real.
- We make the way into it clearer.

## Depth underneath. Clarity on the surface.

Someone should not have to understand the architecture in order to feel the idea. The
governed concepts remain intellectual property; public surfaces translate them.

| Governed term | Public translation |
|---|---|
| Authority | Who gets to decide? |
| Conditions | What is making this harder or easier? |
| Structural ceiling | Some things cannot be changed by one person alone. |
| Recalibration | When life changes, the plan can change too. |
| Sovereignty | A life that is actually yours. (context-dependent) |

Where useful, show the governed term quietly beneath the plain statement:

> When life changes, the plan can change too.
> *The Recalibration Loop*

## Repeatable public phrases

Built to be remembered after one encounter, and quoted without a glossary.

- Less fixing. More living.
- Change the conditions. Not the person.
- What is making this harder?
- Who gets to decide?
- Behaviour is information, not a verdict.
- Support should make more life possible.
- When life changes, the plan can change too.
- You still get a say in what happens next.

## Diagrams

Banned: any diagram that requires the reader to ask "what do these circles represent?"
The meaning must be legible before any animation. Label with the question the diagram
answers, then name the governed term underneath. Every diagram must survive as text.

## Accessibility is a visible brand quality

Not a badge in the footer - something people feel. Generous whitespace and short reading
chunks; no forced animation or autoplay; obvious navigation; strong contrast and readable
type; no meaning carried by colour alone; descriptive links rather than "Learn more"; a
clear hierarchy on every screen; optional depth rather than compulsory depth; captions,
transcripts and real image descriptions; no scrolling tricks that hide information; no
experience that demands perfect attention, memory or processing speed.

And: the accessible version must not be visually boring. Accessible can still be
editorial, rebellious and unmistakable.

## The brief

Make it look world-class without making people decode world-classness. Beautiful enough
to feel like a movement. Clear enough to understand while exhausted. Deep enough that
professionals can keep digging. Human enough that people recognise themselves before they
meet the framework.
