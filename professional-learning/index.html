Acknowledgement of Country

Sovereignty was never ceded. No treaty was ever signed. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples are the Traditional Custodians of the lands and waters on which this work is made, and that does not become less true with time, legislation or silence.

FREE 2B ME acknowledges Elders past, present and emerging. This body of work exists because systems cause harm when they ask people to become easier to accommodate. No community in this country has been asked to do that more often, or for longer, than First Nations peoples.

We hold that as a matter of practice, not sentiment.

Professional Learning

Learn to make more room for the person.

Good intentions matter. So do the conditions people actually encounter.

Professional learning at FREE 2B ME develops the judgement to notice what is happening, question what has been assumed, make access possible and support people without making conformity the hidden goal.

Values matter. People experience them through practice.

Support does not cancel capability

None of these are contradictions.

  • Brilliant and needs processing time.
  • Capable and needs written communication.
  • Reliable and has fluctuating capacity.
  • Committed and needs recovery.
  • Skilled and needs support.

Capability with support is still capability.

A person’s needs do not erase what they can do. And what they can do does not erase what it may cost.

Capacity is not a character test.

What somebody managed yesterday tells us something about yesterday. It does not create a contract for today.

Capacity can shift with health, pain, sleep, processing load, communication, relationships, support, environment, demands and current circumstances. Variation is information, not a moral verdict.

Teams that understand this stop using words like lazy, unreliable, unmotivated or not trying as substitutes for inquiry. They start asking better questions.

Inclusion is not something you can declare.

A policy can use all the right language and still leave people unable to participate. Inclusion becomes visible in ordinary moments.

  1. Can somebody understand what is happening?
  2. Can they communicate in a way that works for them?
  3. Can they ask for help?
  4. Can they disagree?
  5. Can they take longer?
  6. Can they participate differently?
  7. Can they need support without losing authority?
  8. Can they contribute without disappearing inside the effort?

Belonging should not require disappearing.

That is not only a values question. It is a practice-design question.

What professional learning develops

Six things that have to change in the work itself.

  1. See more accurately

    Distinguish what was observed from what was inferred. Notice hidden demands, access conditions, support already working and what remains unknown.

  2. Reason more carefully

    Hold skills, health, communication, relationships, environment and systems in the same account without deciding in advance where the answer belongs.

  3. Make access possible

    Ask what needs to be available before a person can understand, communicate, participate, choose or show what they can do.

  4. Use power well

    Support choice without abandoning professional responsibility. Make room for disagreement, uncertainty, supported decision-making and changing one’s mind.

  5. Act where there is leverage

    Sometimes useful action belongs with the person. Sometimes with a practitioner. Sometimes with a relationship, workplace, service or system. Often several things can happen at once.

  6. Review what the work actually made possible

    Not: did the person comply? But: did this create more access, more choice, more participation, more capability or more of the life that matters to them?

Two questions to take into practice.

  1. What does my practice require the person to become?
  2. Does this leave room for the person to be themselves?

Neither replaces clinical judgement, evidence, safeguarding, treatment or professional responsibility. They help expose the hidden conditions inside apparently good practice.

What this looks like

Three ways this arrives in real work.

  • WorkshopsFor teams that keep encountering the same difficulty and want to examine what they may be missing—not simply acquire another script.
  • Practice developmentReasoning, formulation, communication, access, decision-making and review translated into real work.
  • Organisational learningWhat the service, environment, documentation, workforce and systems are asking people to carry.
